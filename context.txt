1. Core Idea

The project is a doctor-facing AI medical copilot that acts as an intelligent clinical assistant throughout a patient's care.

Its most important characteristic is that it remembers the patient.

Rather than treating every consultation as an isolated conversation, the system maintains a structured, longitudinal representation of the patient's medical history and connects:

previous consultations;
diagnoses;
symptoms;
medications;
allergies;
laboratory results;
medical images;
procedures;
treatment outcomes;
follow-ups;
clinical notes.

When a doctor encounters a patient, the system retrieves the relevant history and combines it with the patient's current clinical information.

Multiple specialized AI agents then analyze different aspects of the case and collaboratively produce a proposed clinical decision.

This may include:

possible diagnosis/differential diagnosis;
recommended investigations;
proposed medication;
dosage/duration where supported by the underlying medical knowledge;
potential contraindications;
drug interactions;
relevant previous treatment outcomes;
follow-up recommendations;
supporting clinical evidence.

The doctor remains in control.

The workflow is:

AI proposes → Doctor reviews → Doctor approves/modifies/rejects → Final decision is recorded.

2. The Problem

The system addresses several problems in current clinical workflows.

2.1 Fragmented patient information

Medical information is often distributed across different documents and visits.

A doctor may need to manually examine:

previous prescriptions;
laboratory reports;
imaging reports;
consultation notes;
diagnoses;
medication history.

Important information can therefore be missed.

Your system creates a longitudinal patient memory that connects these pieces.

2.2 Current consultations don't contain the complete patient context

A current symptom can only be interpreted properly when relevant historical information is available.

For example:

Patient currently presents with chest discomfort.

The system might retrieve:

previous cardiac investigations;
previous medication;
previous abnormal laboratory results;
previous similar symptoms;
previous diagnosis.

The AI can then tell the doctor:

"The patient experienced a similar episode in 2025, during which X was observed and treatment Y was initiated."

This is much more valuable than a chatbot that only sees today's consultation.

Your project isn't primarily:

"An AI that diagnoses diseases."

There are already many systems that do that.

Your stronger proposition is:

An AI system that maintains and reasons over a patient's longitudinal multimodal medical memory to generate context-aware clinical decisions.

36. The Proposal's Central Narrative

If you remember only one thing while writing the proposal, make the story:

Healthcare information is fragmented, longitudinal, multimodal, and difficult to reason over. Existing AI systems often focus on isolated tasks or individual consultations. This project proposes a doctor-in-the-loop, multi-agent medical copilot that maintains longitudinal patient memory, integrates heterogeneous clinical information, retrieves relevant medical evidence, performs specialized analyses, and generates proposed diagnoses, investigations, and treatments for physician review and approval.

That is the core of the proposal.

And I would make patient memory the research centerpiece, while diagnosis, X-ray analysis, medication recommendations, RAG, etc. are capabilities built around that memory layer.

That keeps the project from becoming "yet another medical chatbot" while still giving you enough AI/ML/multi-agent work to make it a substantial FYP.
