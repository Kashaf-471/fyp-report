\chapter{Project Vision}
A physician needs to connect the patient's current concerns with information from earlier encounters. Notes, imaging, laboratory information and medication context may each explain a different part of the case, but their value depends on their integration. Zeest is designed to provide a single workflow for that review, from patient selection through consultation capture, specialised analysis and clinical draft review. Longitudinal memory supplies relevant historical context, while evidence retrieval provides supporting clinical material. The physician remains responsible for determining whether the resulting recommendation should be accepted, changed or rejected. The intended value is a coherent workspace in which the relationships between inputs, findings, evidence and decisions remain visible. Achieving that value requires a defined scope and coordinated processing responsibilities rather than the addition of unrelated features. The project focuses on integrating existing capabilities into a reviewable clinical workflow and investigating how its memory and reasoning components contribute to the resulting recommendations.

\section{Problem Domain Overview}
Zeest operates in clinical decision support, where information about a patient is prepared and analysed to assist a physician's assessment. The physician begins by viewing the patient list, selecting a patient and opening the relevant encounter. Historical notes and available clinical information provide context for the current consultation. Consultation audio is converted into structured notes, which the physician can review and correct before further processing. Supported imaging and laboratory inputs, together with medication context, contribute additional information to the case. The Clinical Case Agent organises these inputs alongside the history retrieved by the Patient Memory Agent. Specialist agents analyse their respective input types, and the Evidence and RAG Agent retrieves relevant guidelines. The Clinical Reasoning Agent then produces a draft diagnosis and treatment plan for critic review and physician inspection. Approval, modification and rejection are separate actions, with only approved content recorded as a clinical decision. Audit history allows the physician to inspect the actions associated with the encounter.

\section{Problem Statement}
Zeest addresses the difficulty of connecting longitudinal patient information, current consultation content and different clinical input types within a decision-support workflow. Reviewing an isolated note or image does not explain how that input relates to earlier encounters, other findings or the patient's medication context. Manual preparation of consultation notes adds a documentation task before the information can be considered alongside those records. A generated recommendation is difficult to review without its supporting evidence and processing context. The system must therefore connect information preparation, historical retrieval, specialised analysis and evidence-supported reasoning while preserving the distinction between an automated draft and an approved clinical decision. Zeest addresses this problem through hybrid memory and coordinated agents, with critic review preceding physician judgement. The research investigates the contribution of that integration by comparing defined system variants. Its purpose is to establish whether the connected workflow offers useful decision support while ensuring that the physician retains authority over recorded content.

\section{Problem Elaboration}
Continuity across encounters requires more than stored records. Historical information must be associated with the correct patient and retain its originating encounter. The current case may require specific details from that history rather than every available record, making relevant retrieval an important part of the workflow. Relational storage provides explicit patient, encounter and source relationships, while semantic retrieval supports the selection of context related to the current information need. The retrieved material must retain its source so that the physician can distinguish a historical observation from a finding produced during the current consultation. In Zeest, these responsibilities belong to the hybrid memory layer and the Patient Memory Agent. Their output becomes part of the prepared clinical case rather than an independent final recommendation. This relationship between storage, retrieval and case preparation is essential to examining whether longitudinal context contributes to recommendation quality. It also requires consistent patient identifiers throughout the processing stages.

Different clinical inputs introduce a second integration challenge. Consultation audio must first become readable notes, whereas an image requires model-specific processing. Laboratory information and medication context have their own representation and analysis requirements. Combining these inputs without preserving their origin would obscure which findings support a recommendation and whether a result applies to the case. Zeest therefore assigns specialised responsibilities to the Clinical Case, Imaging, Laboratory and Medication Safety agents. The case organisation stage connects the available inputs, and each specialist contributes findings relevant to its own role. Missing or unsupported inputs must remain distinguishable from successfully analysed information. The physician needs to inspect the notes and findings before treating them as useful context for a clinical draft. This arrangement makes multimodal integration a coordinated process rather than a simple collection of files. It also provides a basis for comparing recommendations produced from text alone with those produced using the supported combination of input types.

Generated reasoning also requires evidence and accountable review. Retrieved passages must be identifiable so that the physician can examine their relationship to the diagnosis and treatment draft. The Safety and Critic Agent adds a review stage before presentation, but the clinical decision still belongs to the physician. Approval accepts specific content for recording, modification changes the draft without automatically approving it, and rejection excludes that draft from approved decisions. These outcomes must remain distinct even when the system retains the draft and its associated review history. The audit log provides a record of the physician's actions and their context, while the approved decision becomes part of the patient's longitudinal information. This separation is necessary to avoid confusing the generation of a recommendation with acceptance of that recommendation. It also connects the review workflow to the research questions, since evidence support and critic findings must be examined alongside the recommendations produced by each system variant.

\section{Goals and Objectives}
The goal of Zeest is to develop a physician-controlled medical copilot that connects patient history, consultation documentation and multimodal clinical findings through a coordinated reasoning workflow. The system combines relational and semantic memory with specialised agents, existing diagnostic models and retrieved evidence. Its output is a reviewable diagnosis and treatment draft, followed by a clear physician decision and an associated audit history. The objectives in Table~\ref{tab:kashaf:objectives} cover the capabilities needed to achieve this goal and evaluate their contribution. O1 and O2 establish the information foundation, O3 through O7 organise analysis and review, and O8 preserves physician control over the recorded decision. O9 connects those capabilities to the research comparison. The objectives are interdependent: retrieving history is useful only when it informs the correct case, and producing a draft is useful only when its findings and supporting evidence can be inspected. Their integration therefore defines both the development direction and the questions addressed by the evaluation.

\section{Project Scope}
The physician-facing workflow includes account access, patient selection, encounter review, consultation recording and the handling of supported clinical inputs. Physicians can inspect and correct structured consultation notes, supply imaging and laboratory information, and review medication context. The system connects these inputs with longitudinal history, specialised findings and retrieved guidelines to generate a diagnosis and treatment draft. Critic review precedes presentation, and the physician can approve, modify or reject the content while inspecting its supporting evidence. Approved decisions and review actions have separate roles in the stored information. The technical scope includes the hybrid memory layer and the full named agent workflow, supported by existing diagnostic models and knowledge resources. Clinical coverage depends on domains for which suitable data and validated models are available. Development and evaluation use de-identified or synthetic cases. Account access, patient navigation and note correction support the clinical workflow; they do not introduce a separate administrative application or change the physician's responsibility for the final decision.

The project does not include training new diagnostic models from scratch, autonomous prescribing or autonomous treatment. Zeest supports review and decision making rather than issuing clinical actions without physician approval. Real-world clinical deployment and regulatory approval are outside the project scope, as are emergency and intensive-care systems. Native mobile applications, patient-facing conversational interfaces and the knowledge graph extension are also excluded. Domains without suitable datasets and validated diagnostic models are not included simply because the language model can generate text about them. These boundaries keep the work centred on memory, integration, reasoning and review. The research comparison examines the defined system variants using an agreed set of cases and assessment conditions; it does not establish universal clinical effectiveness. Table~\ref{tab:kashaf:scope} summarises the included capabilities and principal exclusions. The resulting scope provides a consistent basis for specifying user interactions, selecting supporting components and determining which behaviours must be represented in the database and architecture.

Longitudinal memory connects the current consultation with relevant history, while evidence retrieval makes supporting material available alongside the generated draft. Critic findings and explicit approval controls keep the final decision within the physician's judgement. These capabilities provide a practical direction for investigating clinical decision support, with the emphasis on coherent information use rather than autonomous care. \section{Constraints}
Clinical domain coverage is constrained by suitable case data and existing validated models. A model's input requirements and supported labels determine which imaging tasks can be integrated, while the permitted use of a dataset determines which cases can support development and evaluation. Transcription depends on the selected component, language coverage and audio conditions. Guideline retrieval and medication analysis likewise require suitable knowledge resources with clear use conditions. The agent workflow also depends on consistent interfaces across the backend, language model, imaging components and memory layer. LangGraph and Google ADK must have an agreed division of responsibilities, and the embedding model must be compatible with the semantic store. Hardware capacity and service access affect processing performance, so numerical targets must be set against a defined environment. Physician approval remains a fixed behavioural constraint regardless of those choices. Together, these dependencies determine the supported operating conditions and resources for reproducible comparison of system variants.

\section{Business Opportunity}
Zeest's value lies in bringing case preparation, clinical context and recommendation review into a physician-facing workflow. Consultation notes, historical information, specialist findings and supporting guidelines can be presented against the same encounter instead of requiring the physician to interpret them as disconnected outputs. The intended benefit is a more coherent review process in which the source of information and the status of a recommendation remain visible. The integration approach also offers a reusable foundation for connecting existing models and knowledge resources to patient memory and physician decisions. For researchers and developers, this allows examination of which components provide useful support and which add complexity. Future adoption would depend on evidence of that value, appropriate operating arrangements and the requirements of the intended setting. Within the project, the opportunity is therefore expressed through the usefulness of the coordinated workspace and the investigation of its components, rather than through an assumed pricing model, customer agreement or commercial deployment.

\section{Stakeholders Description/ User Characteristics}
Physicians use Zeest and interpret the information presented during patient review. They need clear patient and encounter context, readable consultation notes and an understandable relationship between findings, supporting evidence and the generated draft. Their clinical knowledge supports the final decision, while familiarity with a web interface is sufficient for the intended navigation and input tasks. Patients are affected by the handling of their clinical information and by the recommendations reviewed by the physician. Their interests include appropriate information use and professional judgement over recorded decisions. The student team develops the integration and maintains consistency between requirements, processing components and data relationships. The supervisor and academic panel assess the problem, research questions, technical decisions and evaluation approach. These stakeholders have different responsibilities, but their interests meet in the need for a clear and accountable workflow. Patient participation does not require a patient-facing conversational interface, and academic stakeholders do not automatically become additional clinical application users.

\subsection{Key High-Level Goals and Problems of Stakeholders}
Physicians need to understand the current encounter without losing the context supplied by earlier visits and other clinical inputs. Their principal challenges are the preparation of consultation information, the review of findings from different components and the assessment of whether retrieved evidence supports a generated recommendation. Patients have an interest in the correct association of information with their case and in decisions being reviewed by the responsible physician. The development team must connect the components without creating contradictory patient context, unsupported outputs or ambiguous decision states. Academic reviewers need a comparison that makes the contribution of memory, agents, critic review and multimodal inputs assessable. Zeest addresses these needs through an integrated workspace, explicit information relationships and a controlled review process. The resulting vision connects the clinical problem to the objectives and scope, establishing the behaviours that the requirements must express. It also identifies the resource and integration constraints that shape the methodology and design developed in later chapters.
