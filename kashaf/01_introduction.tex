\chapter{Introduction}

Patient information accumulates across consultations, laboratory investigations, medical images and medication records. These sources describe different parts of a patient's condition, but reviewing them separately can leave the current encounter disconnected from earlier findings. A physician needs relevant historical context alongside the information collected during the present consultation. Patient and encounter relationships provide the basis for organising that context.\cite{zeestA:mimicIV} Consultation documentation creates another demand: the spoken exchange must become readable notes before it can be considered with other inputs. Zeest addresses these connected needs through longitudinal patient memory, real-time transcription and a coordinated clinical workflow that keeps the physician responsible for the final decision.

Zeest is an agentic medical copilot that combines patient history, transcribed notes, imaging, laboratory information and medication context. Specialised agents prepare the case and analyse supported inputs using existing validated models and knowledge resources. The Evidence and RAG Agent retrieves clinical guidelines with citations, and the Clinical Reasoning Agent generates a diagnosis and treatment draft. The Safety and Critic Agent reviews this draft before physician presentation. The physician can inspect the findings, supporting evidence and critic review, then approve, modify or reject the draft. Only explicitly approved content enters the clinical record, while audit history preserves the review actions associated with each encounter.

\section{Purpose of this Document}

Zeest investigates how longitudinal memory, agent coordination, evidence retrieval and critic review can support clinical decision making. The central comparison examines a single language model, a memory-augmented variant and the full system, with controlled contrasts for critic and multimodal contributions. The requirements and design establish the physician workflow, component responsibilities and information relationships needed for these comparisons. Existing validated diagnostic models form the supporting groundwork; the research focuses on their integration with memory and review. Clinical coverage depends on suitable data and models, and evaluation uses de-identified or synthetic cases. The intended outcome is an assessable decision-support system whose recommendations remain subject to physician judgement.

\section{Intended Audience}

The primary audience includes the project supervisor, academic evaluators and the student development team. Evaluators need a clear account of the clinical problem, research contribution and technical decisions, while developers need consistent requirements and component responsibilities. Physicians are also relevant readers because Zeest centres on their interpretation of patient information and review of generated recommendations. The descriptions of findings, evidence and approval controls should therefore remain understandable without requiring knowledge of agent orchestration. Researchers interested in clinical language models, memory and retrieval can examine how these capabilities are integrated. The writing connects technical responsibilities to user interactions so that each audience can follow the system's intended behaviour.
